This policy explains what Scanly does with your information. It applies only to the Scanly Android application published by Umesh Ghimire under the package name com.mantrabrain.scanly, and to no other app or website.

The short version

  • Scanly has no account and no sign-up. It never asks for your name, email address, phone number, date of birth or postal address, and there is nowhere in the app to enter them.
  • Your scans stay on your phone. Scan history, the codes you create, your notes and your tags are stored only in local storage on your device. They are never uploaded to the developer or to anyone else.
  • Camera images are never uploaded. Frames are analysed on the device and discarded; no photograph is stored or transmitted.
  • Scanly shows ads through Google AdMob, which uses an advertising identifier. This is the only routine collection of any kind, and it is described in full below.
  • You can delete everything from inside the app at any time, without contacting anyone.

1. Information Scanly collects

1.1 Information you provide

None. Scanly has no registration, no login, no profile and no contact form. It does not collect personal information such as your name, email address, phone number or address.

Some features let you type text that is then stored on your device only — for example the content of a QR code you are creating, a note you attach to a scan, or a tag name. If you choose to put personal details into a QR code you are designing (a contact card, for instance), that text is saved in Scanly's local database on your phone and is not sent anywhere.

1.2 Information collected automatically

WhatCollected byWhy
Advertising ID (Android AD_ID) Google AdMob To select and measure ads, and to limit how often the same ad is shown.
Device and app information used for ads (device type, operating system version, coarse region, IP address as part of the ad request) Google AdMob Standard ad delivery and fraud prevention, handled by Google's SDK.
Your consent choices for advertising Google User Messaging Platform Stored on your device so your choice is remembered and respected.

Scanly itself contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising network other than AdMob, and no tracking pixels. The developer does not operate a server and receives no data about you.

1.3 Information Scanly deliberately does not collect

  • Your scan history, and the contents of codes you scan or create.
  • Photographs, or any camera image.
  • Your contacts, calendar, location or files.
  • Your Wi-Fi passwords, including those read from a Wi-Fi QR code.
  • Biometric data. If you enable the optional lock on your history, the fingerprint or face check is performed entirely by Android; Scanly is told only whether the check succeeded.

2. Permissions, and why each one exists

PermissionPurpose
CAMERA To read QR codes and barcodes. The camera runs only while the scanning screen is open, and is released when Scanly goes to the background.
INTERNET To request ads, and for the optional short-link check described in section 4.
VIBRATE To give a short buzz when a code is detected.
com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID Required by Google Play for apps that use an advertising identifier.

Scanly does not request storage, photo-library, location, contacts, microphone or calendar permissions. Scanning a picture from your gallery uses the Android system photo picker, which hands Scanly the single image you select and gives it no access to the rest of your photos. Saving a contact or adding a calendar event from a scanned code opens the relevant Android app with the details pre-filled — you complete the action there, and Scanly never reads your contacts or calendar.

3. Where your data is stored

Scan history, generated codes, tags, notes, counting sessions and your settings are held in a database and a settings file inside Scanly's private application storage on your device. Other apps cannot read them. Nothing in that database is transmitted off the device by Scanly.

You can move your own data yourself, and only when you ask for it:

  • Export your history as a CSV or JSON file, or create an encrypted backup protected by a passphrase you choose. The file is then handed to Android's share sheet and goes wherever you send it. The passphrase is never stored or transmitted — if you lose it, nobody, including the developer, can open the file.
  • Import a CSV or JSON file you select, to bring in history from another scanner app. The file is read on the device only.

4. Network use by the safety features

Scanly's safety checks run offline on your device. One optional check uses the network, and a second exists in the code but is switched off in the released app. Both are described here so nothing is hidden from you.

4.1 Following shortened links

A shortened link hides where it really goes, which is how many QR code scams work. When you scan one, Scanly can follow it to reveal the true destination.

This runs only for a fixed list of well-known shortening services (such as bit.ly, t.co, tinyurl.com, goo.gl and similar). An ordinary scanned address is never sent anywhere. The request is a minimal HEAD request that carries no cookies, no referrer, no identifier and no scan history — only the shortened link itself, sent to the shortening service that issued it. You can switch this off in Settings.

4.2 Reputation lookup — not active in the current release

Scanly contains support for optionally checking a scanned address against Google Safe Browsing. It is not enabled in the version published on Google Play: the feature requires an API key that is not included in the released build, so the option does not appear in Settings and no scanned address is ever sent to Google Safe Browsing.

It is described here so this policy stays accurate if it is switched on in a future version. If that happens it will remain off unless you turn it on, because it is the one feature that would send a scanned address to a third party. Were you to enable it, the address you scanned would be sent to Google for checking and handled under Google's Privacy Policy. Everything else in Scanly's safety engine runs offline on your device.

4.3 The privacy dashboard

Because these are the kind of claims that are easy to make and hard to trust, Scanly includes a privacy dashboard under Settings → Privacy that records every network request the app has made, with a running count. It is a measurement, not a promise, and you can check it at any time.

5. Advertising

Scanly is free, and advertising pays for it. Ads are supplied by Google AdMob. Google may use an advertising identifier and related device information to select and measure ads. Google's handling of that data is governed by the Google Privacy & Terms and AdMob's policies.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland or a US state with applicable privacy legislation, Scanly presents a consent form from Google's User Messaging Platform before personalised ads are requested, and your choice is respected and remembered. You can change it later from Settings.

You can reset or delete your advertising identifier at any time in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads, which also lets you opt out of personalised advertising system-wide.

Where ads never appear: Scanly does not place an advert on a scan result, on a safety warning, or over the camera. Watching an optional rewarded advert removes all ads for six hours.

6. Links and actions that leave Scanly

Opening a scanned link, looking up a product barcode with a search provider, calling a scanned number or sending a scanned email hands the action to another app or website. Once you leave Scanly, this policy no longer applies; the privacy policy of the destination does. Scanly never opens a scanned link automatically — you always choose.

7. Children

Scanly is not directed at children. Because it shows advertising, it is rated for users aged 13 and over, and it is not designed or intended for use by children under 13. The developer does not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has provided information through this app, please contact me.umeshuser@gmail.com.

8. Your rights and choices

  • Delete everything. Settings → Delete my scan history removes every scan, note and tag from the device permanently. Uninstalling Scanly also removes all of its local data.
  • Take your data with you. Export it as CSV or JSON, or as an encrypted backup, at any time.
  • Control advertising. Change your consent choice in Settings, or reset your advertising ID in Android Settings.
  • Turn off the link check. Settings → Check where short links go disables the only safety feature in the released app that uses the network.
  • Withdraw camera access at any time in Android Settings. Scanly still works on images you pick.

Because Scanly holds no data about you on any server, there is no account for the developer to look up, correct or erase. Requests under the GDPR, UK GDPR or CCPA/CPRA that concern advertising data should be directed to Google as the party that processes it; anything else can be resolved from within the app using the controls above. You are welcome to write to the address below with any question about this.

9. Data retention

Data stored on your device is kept until you delete it or uninstall the app. The developer retains nothing, because the developer receives nothing. Ad data retention is determined by Google.

10. Security

Scanly's database sits in private application storage that other apps cannot read. Backups you create are encrypted with a passphrase you choose, and that passphrase is never stored or transmitted. You can optionally require a fingerprint or face check before your history can be viewed. No method of electronic storage is completely secure, but because nothing is sent to a server, there is no central store of your scans to be breached.
